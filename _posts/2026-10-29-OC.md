> Suggestions for OC 2<br>
First, turn “same as OC 1” into OC 2’s own fracture.
OC 2 currently shares too much of OC 1’s trauma, relationship structure, and baseline. If OC 2 repeats “high school destroyed her mental health,” she risks becoming OC 1 in a fantasy skin. Ask what OC 2’s trauma specifically is in the new world. A stronger version is this: OC 1’s pain is “my body is not decided by me.” OC 2’s pain can become “my body is changing, but I do not know whether this is what I want.” This makes OC 2 an independent character with her own question, not a mirror of OC 1.<br>
Second, build a causal chain between “wanting OR or coding” and “being held back.”
The current limits—education system failure, mental condition, hardware shortage, color vision deficiency—are listed as parallel labels. A round character needs causality between labels, not just a list. Ask which limitation makes her most angry, not which one is saddest. For example, color vision deficiency may block natural science, but OR is mathematical. Why does she not pursue mathematics? Is it because the system blocks her, or because she no longer believes she deserves mathematics? The second answer creates a character who is not only pressed down by the outside world, but also presses herself down. That is much richer. Also make her cognitive distortion concrete in math and coding: does she over-doubt her proofs, get stuck on one logical step, or see patterns that are not there?<br>
Third, make “mathematics is universal” her only lever and her biggest risk.
The most distinctive world rule is that language fails, but math and science symbols are nearly universal. This should not only make communication convenient. It should also expose her. If math is her only effective way to speak, then every time she uses math, she shows her strongest ability and her most vulnerable place. This creates a dilemma: staying low-profile keeps her safe but lonely; showing ability makes her visible but may repeat the trauma of being attacked. This dilemma is stronger than “she wants OR but lacks hardware.”<br>
Fourth, give the vampire noble a reason that only OC 2 can provide.
The encounter logic already works: the vampire notices her because she sees that the numbers are wrong. To avoid the “strong person recognizes useful subordinate” cliché, decide what the vampire sees in her. Is she a useful tool, or a kind of counterpart? The vampire’s non-human traits—unchanging, long-lived, blood-dependent—can contrast with her “becoming” state: body changing, mind changing, identity changing. Then the relationship has thematic weight: can an unchanging being understand someone who is being forcibly changed? Her real question may not be “will he help me,” but “can something that never changes understand something that is changing?”<br>
Fifth, try a writing exercise.
Do not start with “what OC 2 is.” Start with “the one thing OC 2 least wants the vampire to know.” It does not need to be a scandal. It can be small and specific: she is not sure she can learn OR; she sometimes misses the things that held her down in the real world because at least they were familiar; she fears that becoming strong in this world will make returning to the real world more painful. Write this for yourself, not necessarily for the main text. Its purpose is to give her an inner place that even “being appreciated” cannot touch. With that place, she is not a person waiting to be saved or developed. She is a person with her own boundary.
